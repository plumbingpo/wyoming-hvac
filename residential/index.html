Clear Pricing

Honest, Upfront Pricing

Before we touch a tool, we find out exactly what's wrong. Our technicians check your system's age and tonnage, the refrigerant charge, the heat exchanger and coils, and the condition of your ductwork. Then you get a straightforward recommendation and the complete price up front, whether the job is swapping a weak capacitor or replacing the whole system. No hidden fees, no surprise add-ons.

A lot of HVAC failures send small signals well before the system stops. Watch for rising utility bills, weak airflow at the registers, frost on the copper lines, or a bedroom that never catches up to the thermostat. Behind those symptoms are usually pinhole refrigerant leaks, worn blower bearings, or a capacitor on its way out. An early service call at the first sign often costs far less than a failed compressor.

Not every visit is about a breakdown. Many Wyoming homeowners ask us to evaluate an aging system before it fails, and it's a smart move. With a typical AC or furnace lifespan of 12 to 18 years, replacing on your own schedule beats a rushed swap in the middle of a heat wave. We'll give you a straight answer on how much life your equipment has left.

Seasonal Tune-Ups

Maintenance Plans for Wyoming Homes

Each maintenance visit from Wyoming HVAC is more than a filter change. The technician cleans the coils, verifies the refrigerant charge, tests capacitors and contactors, inspects the burners and heat exchanger, clears the condensate drain, puts in a fresh filter, and measures static pressure across the ducts. Minor faults are caught while they're still cheap to fix, and the equipment keeps running clean and efficient.

We offer flexible maintenance options for homeowners, with spring cooling tune-ups and fall heating tune-ups for most systems. This is especially important in dusty areas, where dirty condenser coils choke heat rejection and drive up energy use. Each home is assessed individually, and the plan is tailored to your equipment's age, your household's comfort needs, and your local climate.

The savings add up faster than most people expect. Clean coils move heat more efficiently, a correct refrigerant charge keeps the compressor from overworking, and a fresh filter spares the blower motor. Compared with the cost of replacing a system even one year early, a maintenance plan is a bargain.

Right-Sized Systems

The Right Size System for Your Home

An undersized system runs nonstop and never catches up on the hottest days, while an oversized one short-cycles, wastes energy, and leaves the house humid. Wyoming HVAC sizes every replacement and new installation to your actual home. That means a proper load calculation that accounts for square footage, insulation, window exposure, and duct layout, rather than simply matching the tonnage of the old unit. For ductless mini-splits, it means matching head capacity to each zone so every room holds its setpoint.

Homes change over time. A finished basement, an addition, or two new bedrooms can outgrow the single-stage system that once kept up just fine. Today's two-stage and variable-speed equipment holds tighter temperatures and uses far less energy. Whether you're comparing a heat pump and a furnace-and-AC pair, or ducted and ductless, we'll lay out the honest pros and cons in SEER2 ratings, operating cost, electrical and venting needs, and installation work.

Common Problems

Heating and Cooling Problems We Solve in Wyoming

Warm air from the vents, a furnace that won't ignite, ice on the indoor coil, leaking refrigerant: every one of these problems has its own cause and calls for its own repair. Wyoming HVAC diagnoses the exact fault before swapping any parts, whether it's a condenser that hums but won't start, a short-cycling furnace, a thermostat that keeps losing its program, a clogged condensate drain, or a mini-split throwing error codes.

Some problems are mostly a nuisance, while others pose real risks. A weak capacitor simply keeps the compressor from starting, but a cracked heat exchanger can leak combustion gases into your living space, and a blocked condensate drain can quietly ruin a ceiling. We match the fix to the failure: capacitors, contactors, and fan motors on cooling systems, igniters, flame sensors, and control boards on furnaces, sealed and repaired ductwork where conditioned air is bleeding into the attic, and a proper refrigerant recharge after a repaired leak. Whatever you're facing, our team has the training and parts to handle it.
